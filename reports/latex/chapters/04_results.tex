\section{Results}
\label{ch:results}

\subsection{ERPs}
\label{sec:res-erp}
\label{sec:res-preproc}
Of the 22,751 correct trials of the final cohort, 90.5\% survived artefact rejection. Targets elicited a large centro-parietal positivity that was absent for standards (Figure~\ref{fig:erp}). At Pz the grand-average target ERP peaked at 484~ms (9.46~µV, SEM 0.50); at 500~ms the target-minus-standard difference was 8.61 (SEM 0.48)~µV. Distractors elicited a smaller positivity that peaked later (504~ms).

\subsection{Classification performance (RQ1)}
\label{sec:res-rq1}
All 16 model, setting and window combinations had a group median balanced accuracy above chance (0.5; Table~\ref{tab:rq1}, Figure~\ref{fig:rq1}). In the \code{full} window, \code{xdawn_rg} had the highest within-participant median (0.949, AUC 0.995), followed by EEGNet (0.941) and \code{lda_tw} (0.939); across participants, EEGNet led (0.924, AUC 0.979). For every fixed model, the group median was lower with \code{early} than with \code{full}. For the full-window leaders, it decreased to 0.873 within participants (\code{xdawn_rg}) and 0.830 across participants (EEGNet). EEGNet had the highest \code{early}-window median in both settings (0.875 and 0.830, respectively). Participant variability was substantial, especially for \code{shallow} within participants in \code{early} (IQR 0.587 to 0.875); some within-participant \code{early} scores fell below chance. These comparisons are descriptive.

\subsection{Classical versus deep learning (RQ2)}
\label{sec:res-rq2}
The best classical model was \code{xdawn_rg} and the best DL model EEGNet in both settings. Within participants they did not differ in balanced accuracy (Holm-corrected $p$ = 0.74), but \code{xdawn_rg} had a higher AUC ($p = 1.0 \times 10^{-5}$). Across participants, EEGNet was better in the \code{full} window on both metrics (balanced accuracy $+0.020$, $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-4}$, $r = 0.75$, better for 28 of 35 participants; AUC $p = 2.4 \times 10^{-5}$); in the \code{early} window the difference was not significant (Table~\ref{tab:rq2}, Figure~\ref{fig:rq2}). EEGNet was also clearly better than ShallowConvNet within participants (better for 34 of 35, $p < 10^{-7}$).

\subsection{What did the models learn? (RQ3)}
\label{sec:res-rq3}
\textbf{Spatial patterns.} In the cross-subject \code{full} models, the most important spatial filter of every fold had a centro-parietal pattern that resembled the P300 map ($|r|$ = 0.61 to 0.91; Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-patterns}). Removing any single filter cost at most 0.018 AUC, so the information is spread over several redundant, mostly P300-like filters.

\textbf{Attributions over time.} Attribution rose from about 250~ms and peaked at 495~ms, before the median response (Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-ig}). After 450~ms the P300 region was more enriched than the motor region (1.20 vs 1.05, $p$ = 0.002), and after most responses (600 to 800~ms) the motor region received no more than an average channel (0.99). The classical LDA showed the same picture: its activation pattern matched the P300 map after 450~ms (spatial correlation 0.99) and was small at C3 (0.14~µV vs 0.55~µV at Pz).

\textbf{Control tasks.} Distractors, which need no response, were decoded from standards almost as well as targets (EEGNet median 0.84 to 0.87 in the \code{full} window), while target vs distractor was hardest (0.68 to 0.72) and lost most in the \code{early} window. Without a response (\code{dis_std}) the motor region received the attribution of an average channel after 250~ms (Table~\ref{tab:rq3-enrichment}). With a response it rose above this baseline between 250 and 600~ms, and in \code{tgt_dis} it became the most attributed region (peak 456~ms, before the median response).

\textbf{Answer to RQ3.} The features learned by EEGNet are interpretable: its decisions rest mainly on the centro-parietal P3b, which the classical LDA also uses. A smaller part comes from left central activity that starts before the button press, most likely motor preparation, and part of it falls inside the \code{early} window.
