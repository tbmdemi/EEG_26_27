\section*{Abstract}
The P300 elicited by rare targets in the oddball paradigm is a classic signal for single-trial EEG decoding. Using the visual three-stimulus oddball task of the Nencki-Symfonia dataset (35 usable participants, 20,600 behaviourally correct epochs after preprocessing), we classified single trials of target versus standard stimuli with two classical pipelines (time-window shrinkage LDA; xDAWN covariances with Riemannian tangent space) and two convolutional networks (EEGNet, ShallowConvNet), within and across participants, in a full (0 to 800~ms) and an early (0 to 450~ms) window. Within participants, xDAWN with tangent space was best (median balanced accuracy 0.949, AUC 0.995) and EEGNet was equivalent in balanced accuracy. Across participants, EEGNet was significantly better than the best classical model (0.924 vs 0.872, Holm-corrected $p < 0.001$). Spatial patterns, Integrated Gradients and LDA activation patterns showed that both model families rely mainly on the centro-parietal P3b, and control tasks revealed a smaller contribution from motor preparation before the button press that the early window does not remove. Deep learning thus helps mainly when data are pooled across people, and its features are interpretable.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} P300, visual oddball, single-trial classification, EEGNet, xDAWN, explainability
