\section*{Summary}
This progress report describes the state of our course project as of 22 November 2026. Using the visual three-stimulus oddball task of the Nencki-Symfonia dataset (35 usable participants, 20,600 behaviourally correct epochs after preprocessing), we classify single trials of target versus standard stimuli with two classical pipelines (time-window shrinkage LDA; xDAWN covariances with Riemannian tangent space) and two convolutional networks (EEGNet, ShallowConvNet), within and across participants, in a full (0 to 800~ms) and an early (0 to 450~ms) window. Preprocessing, ERP analysis, all model runs, the RQ2 statistics and the model explanations are complete. Preliminary results: within participants, xDAWN with tangent space was best (median balanced accuracy 0.949, AUC 0.995) and EEGNet was equivalent in balanced accuracy; across participants, EEGNet was significantly better than the best classical model (0.924 vs 0.872, Holm-corrected $p < 0.001$). Spatial patterns, Integrated Gradients and LDA activation patterns indicate that both model families rely mainly on the centro-parietal P3b, with a smaller contribution from motor preparation before the button press. The remaining work is mainly figures, quality-control plots, a decision on two reprocessed participants and team review (Section~\ref{ch:conclusion}).

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} P300, visual oddball, single-trial classification, EEGNet, xDAWN, explainability
